# Changelog

## Not yet released

"Open my Downloads folder" opens the folder, and a Settings page's translated name is found when said with an article or a settings word round it.

Two changes reach what 0.9.0 already did:

- Apps: "Open my Downloads folder" opens your Downloads folder in your file manager, where 0.9.0 said no app had that name and offered to look for a folder. Your Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Music, Pictures, Videos, Templates and Public folders and your home folder open by name, wherever GLib says they are: by the English name, by the name the folder really has, or by a Spanish, Portuguese, Chinese or Arabic name. Only those open, never another folder or a path. An app named exactly the same, such as GNOME's Music, still wins unless you say "folder". A folder opens the way `file_open` opens one, with no card, and only inside your home folder and outside hidden folders. Not yet tried on a real desktop.
- Apps: a Settings page's name, in English or as Settings shows it in your language, is found when said with an article or a word for settings or for a page round it, as in "la configuración del sonido", "o painel de som", "إعدادات الصوت", "声音设置" or "Einstellungen für Klang", and whatever its case, accents, spaces or a plural s. In 0.9.0 a translated name had to be said just as Settings shows it, and Spanish's "Ratón y panel táctil" couldn't be found at all, as its "panel" was taken for a settings word. A page's other words, such as "wallpaper", are still English only. Not yet tried on a real desktop.

After updating, restart the daemon with `systemctl --user restart tuxistant`, or log out and back in. The extension is unchanged.

## 0.9.0, a preview

A preview, published as a GitHub prerelease. 0.5.0 stays the stable release and what the website offers.

A small release: 0.8.0 with two changes. Tux no longer reads out escapes in a song's title or artist, or in a player's name. In 0.8.0 the marks round an Arabic or Hebrew title, the start of a terminal's colour code and the joiners inside a joined emoji were each read out as a backslash, a u and a number; now what nobody sees is left out, and joined emoji are kept whole. And with no mail in Thunderbird, `tuxistant doctor`'s line about mail search says a search reaches at most 3 of the accounts in Online Accounts, which is as many as a search has reached since 0.7.0. Nothing else in what Tux does has changed.

Neither change has been tried on a real desktop. The change to titles has only run in tests, with stand-in players, one of them a made-up player on a private bus, and never with a real one: no real player has sent such a title yet, and nobody has heard how the voice says one. The doctor line has only run in tests too, with made-up settings and a made-up Thunderbird folder. Checks 19 and 16 under "Check on the real desktop" in docs/ROADMAP.md are their first real runs. Nothing 0.7.0 added has been tried on a real desktop, a real Online Accounts account or a real server yet either: see 0.7.0 below.

The rest changes only the docs, a test and the website. docs/ROADMAP.md's limit on media titles now says what is said and what isn't known, among them that the voice reads a title as English, and docs/SKILLS.md says what is left out of a title, where it said control characters are shown as escapes. docs/ROADMAP.md also lists a limit found since: `tuxistant doctor` stops with a Python error, rather than naming the package, when python3-websockets is missing, as it has since 0.3.0, though `./install.sh` names it. A new test runs doctor in a Python without dbus_fast, which it names. On the website, 0.8.0's chip in Spanish, Portuguese and Chinese now says what the English one does: that the doctor command, written as it is typed, explains mail search.

Two changes reach what 0.8.0 already did:

- Media: a song's title and artist, and a player's name, no longer have escapes read out in them. In 0.8.0 the right-to-left marks and isolates round an Arabic or Hebrew title, the start of a terminal's colour code and the joiners inside a joined emoji were each read out as an escape: a backslash, a u and a number. Now the marks are left out, as are other characters nobody sees, some of which 0.8.0 kept as they were, and a terminal's codes for a colour, a window title or a link. Joined emoji, such as a family, a heart on fire or Scotland's flag, are kept whole, and a long title is cut between them rather than inside one. A title made only of characters nobody sees counts as no title. Only the text has changed: the voice still reads a title as English, so one in Arabic or Hebrew may come out wrong or not at all. Not yet tried with a real player.
- `tuxistant doctor`'s "mail search available" line, with "Search my mail" on and no mail in Thunderbird, says a search goes to at most 3 of the Google or Microsoft accounts in Online Accounts whose Mail switch is on: `at most 3 of them`. A search has stopped at three since 0.7.0, and 0.8.0's line didn't say so. Not yet tried on a real desktop.

After updating, restart the daemon with `systemctl --user restart tuxistant`, or log out and back in. The extension is unchanged. `tuxistant doctor` has its new line straight away.

## 0.7.0, a preview

A preview, published as a GitHub prerelease. 0.5.0 stays the stable release and what the website offers.

If you use Tuxistant, two things are new. Mail search no longer needs Thunderbird: with "Search my mail" on in Settings and no mail in Thunderbird, Tux searches a Google or Microsoft account you added in GNOME Settings under Online Accounts, on that account's own mail server, and reads the answer out as before. Whether Microsoft's mail server takes the token Online Accounts hands out isn't known yet, so a Microsoft account may not work at all. The switch starts off, as it has since 0.3.0, and while it is off no mail is read, from Thunderbird or from Online Accounts. Tux uses no password for it: it asks Online Accounts for an access token that lasts about an hour, once per search, and leaves an account set up with a password alone. Through Online Accounts your question's words go to each connected account's mail server with no card, even after the conversation has read something private, which is the one exception to the rule that nothing then leaves your own server without a click. A question about your own server, such as "What's running on my server?" or "Is LocalAI running on my home server?", now asks the server in Settings over ssh, with your own key and one fixed command that reads, where 0.6.0 looked on this computer and said no. Tux says what docker and systemd list there, in their own words, and doesn't judge whether an app works. Nothing needs switching on: it uses the server in Settings and your own ssh key, but only a key that works with no passphrase and no agent, on a server whose host key you have accepted once in a terminal. Any other key, or a host key not yet accepted, gets a sentence saying so rather than an answer.
